# Macroeconomic analysis protocol

## Releases and revisions

Record geography, source agency, release timestamp/timezone, vintage, seasonality, real/nominal units and previous/comparable periods. Initial GDP, revised GDP and latest GDP are different information sets; a strategy backtest must use *point-in-time* historical releases.

## Key blocks

**Output:** GDP expenditure identity `Y=C+I+G+(X-M)`; real versus nominal output; potential output and gap are model estimates.

**Prices:** headline/core CPI, PCE, GDP deflator, monthly annualized versus YoY, breadth and services persistence. A decline in YoY inflation does not imply falling prices.

**Labor:** payroll establishment survey vs household unemployment survey, participation, hours, wages, claims and JOLTS. A seemingly weak print may partly reflect revisions, strikes or seasonal composition.

**Policy:** policy rate path, effective overnight rates, reserve liquidity, QT/QE, fiscal supply and term premium; avoid saying a central bank 'sets' every yield.

**External sector:** BoP current/financial accounts, FX conventions, net international investment positions, hedged returns, covered interest parity and cross-currency basis. Distinguish currency translation risk from default/funding risk.

## Explain market reaction without hindsight

Markets trade the **surprise relative to expectations**, future policy path and risk premium, not an unconditional mapping such as 'bad payrolls = bonds rally'. Check multiple hypotheses: growth shock, inflation shock, policy repricing, positioning, liquidity shock. Confidence should reflect actual release and futures/curve evidence.
