# Fundamental and valuation guide

## Always normalize statements first

Use matching periods, currency, accounting standard, segment and consolidation perimeter; reconcile trailing twelve months against fiscal-year and quarter reports. Check lease accounting, stock compensation, one-offs, capitalized costs, goodwill, working capital and noncontrolling interests.

## A working bridge

`Revenue → Gross profit → EBIT → NOPAT → FCFF`. Typical unlevered free cash flow: `FCFF=EBIT(1-T)+D&A-CapEx-ΔNWC`. A company with impressive EBITDA can still consume cash through working capital and maintenance CapEx.

## Valuation

- `EV=market_cap+debt+preferred+minority_interest-cash` is a *starting convention*; make lease and pension adjustments explicit.
- For unlevered FCFF discount with WACC. For dividends/equity cash flows discount with cost of equity. Never mix cash-flow type and discount rate.
- `TV=FCFF_(n+1)/(WACC-g)` assumes stable `g < WACC`; model stability, capital reinvestment and terminal margins.
- Multiple comparables need sector, growth, profitability, cash conversion, leverage and accounting consistency.
- DCF results are assumption distributions, not precise intrinsic truths. Sensitize discount rates, margins and terminal values.

## Bank-specific economics

Do not value deposit-taking institutions with industrial EV/EBITDA logic without careful rationale. Use NIM, cost of risk, normalized provisions, CET1, RWA, LCR/NSFR, FTP allocation, recurring fees, liquidity options and cost/income. Transfer-pricing margin is not the same as fully allocated after-tax profit.

## Key traps

Use average balance sheet denominators where appropriate; reconcile consolidated vs segment numbers, and distinguish organic growth from acquisitions. Negative equity, cyclical earnings and net-debt definitions can make ratios economically misleading.
